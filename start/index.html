Set up PixFray on your Twitch channel

Viewers pick a pixel fighter on this site and duel each other with chat commands. The fights play out on your stream through an OBS browser source.

Sign in with the Twitch account you stream on

PixFray turns on for that channel, then the Stream setup page opens. Twitch asks you to allow reading your moderator list.

Sign in with Twitch

Setup takes about 10 minutes, after one Twitch sign-in

You need OBS and the StreamElements bot in your chat. After you sign in, the Stream setup page checks off each step as it starts working.

  1. Add the overlay to OBS Make it a Browser Source at 1920×1080. The setup page gives you the link.
    Browser Source · URLpixfray.xyz/overlay.html?channel=youWidth 1920 · Height 1080
  2. Turn off the Duel module It's a built-in StreamElements module. With it off, only one bot answers duel commands.
    Duel modulePixFray commandsOne bot answers each duel
  3. Paste 7 commands into StreamElements Each reply is ready to copy: !challenge, !fight, !decline, !rematch, !elo, !ranks and !fray. You can rename them.
    !challengeCopy replykai !challenge @rinStreamElements @kai challenges @rin! @rin, type !fight to fight.
  4. Let your moderators help Optional They can manage fighters, ranks and settings.
    Your moderators canExamplePause duelsReset healthReset

PixFray asks Twitch for one permission: your moderator list

What PixFray reads

  • Your public Twitch name, from sign-in
  • Your moderator list (moderation:read), so your mods can open mod controls

What PixFray never does

  • Post in chat as you
  • Change your channel, title or settings
  • See your email or stream key

You can turn PixFray off at any time from the Stream setup page. Fighters and ranks are kept, so turning it back on picks up where you left off.

PixFray is open source

The overlay, the site and the server are MIT-licensed, and the character art is CC0. Read the code, report a bug, or run your own copy on a Cloudflare account.
